Understanding Landscape Connectivity Metrics

Definitions from the Metro Manila Urban Green Space Network Study

Dense Green Patches (3,064 patches)
Contiguous clusters of dense tree canopy and urban vegetation identified across Metro Manila's 17 LGUs from 30 m Landsat 8/9 imagery. Each patch acts as a habitat refuge or stepping stone for urban wildlife.
Dispersal Threshold (100 m, 400 m, 1,000 m)
The maximum edge-to-edge distance an organism can cross through the built-up urban matrix between green spaces. Short thresholds (100 m) reflect gap-sensitive understory species; wider thresholds (400 m and 1,000 m) represent more mobile urban birds.
Functional Links (3,420 / 9,616 / 11,574)
Direct potential movement pathways connecting any two green patches whose edge-to-edge gap is within the selected dispersal threshold—even if a bird flies over intermediate patches without stopping. Higher link counts indicate stronger route redundancy and movement options across the city.
Maximum Link Weight (3.33 / 13.33 / 33.33)
The dispersal distance threshold expressed in 30 m Landsat pixel units (threshold in meters divided by 30 m pixel resolution), matching the graph cost-distance scale used in the study.
Network Components (2,290 / 215 / 15)
Separate, isolated sub-networks of connected patches. Wildlife can move freely among patches inside the same component, but cannot cross gaps separating different components. Fewer components mean a more unified habitat network—at 1,000 m, Metro Manila's 3,064 patches consolidate into just 15 connected components.
Ecological Condition
Overall landscape permeability classification at each dispersal distance: Severely fragmented at 100 m (2,290 isolated clusters), Fragmented at 400 m (215 clusters), and Highly connected at 1,000 m (15 clusters).
